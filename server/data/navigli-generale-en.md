# The Navigli of Milan: A Complete History

## Introduction

The Navigli of Milan constitute one of the most important systems of artificial canals in pre-industrial Europe. Created to connect the city to the major rivers and lakes of Northern Italy, they played a fundamental role for centuries in trade, irrigation, and the economic development of the Milanese territory.

---

## Origins and Early Development (12th–13th Century)

The first hydraulic works date back to the 12th century, when the medieval commune of Milan began developing artificial canals for defensive and agricultural purposes.

The first major canal was the **Naviglio Grande**, whose construction began around 1177 and continued through the 13th century. It diverted water from the Ticino River, supplying water to Milan and the surrounding countryside.

### Initial Functions

* Irrigation of agricultural land
* Defense of the city (moats and natural barriers)
* Early forms of local transport

---

## Expansion of the Navigli System (14th–15th Century)

During the era of the lordships, particularly under the Visconti and later the Sforza dynasties, the Navigli system was greatly expanded.

### Major Works

* Naviglio Pavese (begun in the 14th century, completed later)
* Naviglio della Martesana (1457)
* Darsena of Milan (city harbor)

The objective was to transform Milan into a commercial hub connected to:

* the Po River
* the Lombard lakes
* the Adriatic Sea

---

## The Contribution of Leonardo da Vinci (15th Century)

During the rule of **Ludovico Sforza**, Milan became a center of engineering innovation.

**Leonardo da Vinci** studied and improved the system of **navigation locks**, making it possible to overcome differences in water level between various sections of the canals.

### Main Innovations

* Optimization of lock systems
* Improved navigability
* Greater efficiency in river transport

---

## The Early Modern Era and Peak Development (16th–18th Century)

Between the 16th and 18th centuries, the Navigli reached the height of their economic importance.

### Economic Role

* Transport of goods (grain, marble, timber)
* Commercial connections with Venice and the rest of Europe
* Support for local crafts and industries

One emblematic example was the transportation of the marble used for the construction of the Milan Cathedral, which arrived directly into the city by water.

---

## The Naviglio Pavese and Completion of the System (18th–19th Century)

The Naviglio Pavese was completed in 1819 during Austrian rule, permanently connecting Milan to the Po River.

This achievement made the Navigli network one of the most advanced inland navigation systems in Europe.

---

## Decline (19th–20th Century)

With the arrival of the Industrial Revolution and the development of railways and road transport, the Navigli gradually lost their importance.

### Main Causes

* Greater efficiency of rail transport
* Urban expansion
* Hygiene and public health concerns

---

## The Covering of the Navigli (20th Century)

During the 20th century, many sections of the Navigli within the city were covered and transformed into roads.

### Reasons

* Improvement of urban traffic circulation
* Reduction of sanitary problems
* Modernization of the city

The inner canal ring (*Cerchia dei Navigli*) was almost entirely buried between the 1920s and 1930s.

---

## Present-Day Situation and Urban Revitalization (21st Century)

Today, the following sections remain visible:

* Naviglio Grande
* Naviglio Pavese
* Naviglio della Martesana
* The Darsena (redeveloped waterfront)

### Current Functions

* Tourist attraction
* Cultural and social district
* Urban regeneration area

In recent years, there has been growing discussion about reopening certain covered sections as part of broader urban renewal projects.

---

## Conclusion

The Navigli of Milan represent a remarkable example of historical hydraulic engineering and integrated urban development. Originally created for agricultural and defensive needs, they evolved into a strategic commercial network that contributed decisively to the city’s economic growth.

Despite their decline due to modernization, the Navigli remain a fundamental part of Milan’s identity today, playing a central role in the city’s cultural and tourist life.
